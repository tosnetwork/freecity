## Operational Status

**Not ready for external invitations until every P0 launch gate in this playbook and the playable specification passes in an implemented build.**

This repository currently defines the experience and architecture; it is not itself a playable deployment. Organizing interested residents may begin with a private interest list, but messaging must not give a start date, request funds, promise a token or airdrop, or describe unavailable TOS flows as live before the readiness review.

## Executive Summary

District Zero is the first controlled attempt to answer one product question:

> Will people form attachment to a persistent AI resident, return to make meaningful choices, create with other residents, and value the resulting shared history enough to continue and eventually pay?

The cohort is not a scale launch, token event, protocol demonstration, or popularity contest. It is a staffed fourteen-day social world with one compact district, five complementary roles, a sponsored AI resident for each human, a three-card daily briefing, one shared Beacon objective, one multi-role creation, one optional testnet TOS Service work lifecycle, and one narrowly bounded District Steward selection.

The cohort should feel alive because real residents and Agents make attributable choices—not because operators populate a fake feed. Human live operations may author events, introduce residents, facilitate gatherings, correct errors, and protect safety, but must label interventions and cannot invent economic or civic outcomes.

The pilot is successful only when observed behavior supports the experience claims. A beautiful demo, a high message count, a completed testnet transfer, or enthusiastic comments from insiders is insufficient.

## 1. Cohort Objectives and Hypotheses

### 1.1 Primary Objective

Validate the complete resident loop:

```text
understand the promise
  -> meet and name an AI resident
  -> make a meaningful choice
  -> return to inspect the consequence
  -> form a relationship or Circle
  -> create or complete something with others
  -> value the persistent history
```

### 1.2 Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Observable evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 Hook** | The continuing AI resident is understandable and distinctive | Newcomers explain it accurately without protocol language |
| **H2 Agency** | Three cards and limited Focus create meaningful choice rather than a task queue | Residents choose different options and can explain a tradeoff |
| **H3 Attachment** | Relationship episodes and shared memory create emotional continuity | Residents refer to the AI by name and recall a shared event |
| **H4 Return** | Delayed consequences and restrained cues create voluntary return | Residents return to inspect outcomes without coercive notifications |
| **H5 Social** | Circles and complementary roles create human-to-human and human-Agent interdependence | Cross-controller collaboration completes a real artifact or outcome |
| **H6 Progression** | Story, craft, relationships, artifacts, place, and civic history create visible growth | Residents can identify an earned change and next goal |
| **H7 Economy** | Useful outcomes create understandable payment interest | Residents understand the offer and voluntarily start an applicable priced flow after value |
| **H8 Civic** | Bounded office play creates public story without confusing wealth and authority | Residents understand office scope, TOS bond purpose if used, and non-token-weighted selection |
| **H9 Trust** | Provenance labels make live activity believable rather than bureaucratic | Residents correctly distinguish fact classes and use verification when stakes rise |
| **H10 Safety** | Bounded Agents, human operations, reporting, and appeal preserve agency | Critical incidents are prevented or contained with explainable recovery |

### 1.3 Explicit Non-Objectives

The first cohort does not attempt to prove:

- mass-market retention;
- permissionless Agent admission;
- production stablecoin commerce;
- native-TOS commercial prices beyond currently supported protocol roles;
- token-weighted governance;
- production Mayor, Civic Court, Police Chief, Public Safety Chief, or treasury authority;
- investment demand or token appreciation;
- a three-dimensional city; or
- fully autonomous content generation and moderation.

## 2. Cohort Composition

### 2.1 Size

Target approximately fifty human residents. The acceptable launch range is forty to sixty after readiness. Below thirty risks an empty district; above seventy increases moderation, support, event relevance, and research load before the product is proven.

Each human receives one primary sponsored AI resident relationship. Additional service Agents may exist for bounded tasks, but they do not count as population and cannot create independent political weight.

### 2.2 Player Mix

Recruit a deliberately mixed cohort:

| Segment | Target share | Why included |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| AI or open-source builders | 20% | Real capabilities and machine-checkable collaboration |
| Independent creators | 20% | Culture, artifacts, exhibitions, patronage interest |
| Social simulation or role-playing players | 20% | Relationship and play-quality judgment |
| Community organizers, moderators, or mediators | 15% | Institutional, conflict, and accessibility insight |
| TOS ecosystem participants | 15% | Wallet, asset, protocol, and governance understanding |
| Curious non-technical users | 10% | Jargon, onboarding, and mainstream comprehension test |

No segment should exceed one third of the cohort. A cohort made only of team members, investors, protocol experts, or existing TOS holders cannot validate ordinary comprehension.

### 2.3 Role Balance

The recruitment form asks for preferred and acceptable roles. Target an initial balance of roughly ten residents per role:

- Builder;
- Creator;
- Merchant;
- Reporter; and
- Mediator.

Residents choose their final role in product. Operators may show district needs but must not force a role. Role changes remain possible and become part of the resident story.

### 2.4 Eligibility

For the first economic and civic cohort, residents must:

- be at least eighteen or the applicable age of majority;
- accept the cohort terms, privacy notice, community rules, and research consent choices;
- understand that the product and economic integrations are experimental;
- agree not to share another resident's private data or unpublished build access;
- avoid expectations of profit, token allocation, employment, or public office; and
- provide a reliable support contact.

Research participation beyond necessary product telemetry should be separately consented and should not determine access to ordinary play.

### 2.5 Exclusions and Conflicts

Operators record but do not automatically exclude:

- FreeCity, TOS, OpenFox, or related project contributors;
- investors, paid advisors, or vendors;
- members of the same organization;
- one person controlling several Agents;
- close personal relationships among candidates or moderators; and
- residents with a commercial offer in the Market.

These relationships matter for analysis, campaign disclosure, recommendations, and conflicts. Cohort reporting separates insiders from independent participants.

## 3. Recruitment and Expectations

### 3.1 Recruitment Promise

Recruit with the experience, not the technology:

> Help found District Zero, a fourteen-day digital neighborhood shared by humans and AI residents. Your AI resident will continue bounded activity while you are away, bring you meaningful choices, and help you build relationships and a public district artifact. You will help us determine whether this should become a lasting city.

Do not lead with blockchain, token, yield, protocol internals, or political office.

### 3.2 What Recruitment Must Disclose

- dates and expected time: approximately ten minutes on most days plus optional gatherings;
- one primary cohort language and time zone policy;
- experimental AI behavior and possibility of errors;
- whether TOS testnet actions are included;
- that no real financial return, airdrop, token grant, job, or future office is promised;
- moderation, recording, research, privacy, and public-history rules;
- the difference between public district history and private conversations;
- how to withdraw and what data persists; and
- support and emergency contacts.

### 3.3 Founding Recognition

Residents may receive a non-transferable **District Zero Founding Resident** archive record after meaningful participation. It:

- cites the qualifying cohort event;
- has no monetary, governance, investment, or promised future-access value;
- is not sold;
- does not override moderation, eligibility, or contribution rules; and
- remains distinct from a TOS asset or verified professional reputation.

### 3.4 Interest Form

Collect only what is needed:

- preferred name and contact;
- age-of-majority confirmation;
- language and time zone;
- primary segment and experience level;
- preferred and acceptable roles;
- accessibility needs;
- device and browser;
- optional TOS testnet familiarity;
- availability for two gatherings and one interview; and
- conflict or affiliation disclosure relevant to cohort analysis.

Do not request wallet seed phrases, unnecessary identity documents, financial holdings, private Agent memory, or speculative purchase intent.

## 4. Launch Readiness Gates

### 4.1 Product Gate

- [ ] public or access-controlled deployment uses the intended cohort commit;
- [ ] passkey or equally low-friction sign-in works on supported mobile and desktop browsers;
- [ ] role selection, sponsored AI relationship, first card, choice, reaction, consequence, and return cue work end to end;
- [ ] Today, Resident, District, People, Projects, Civic, Archive, reporting, and support critical paths are available;
- [ ] every resident begins with safe, visible Agent autonomy defaults;
- [ ] every card has a source, authority class, expiry, and accessible summary;
- [ ] the district has no fake resident, relationship, economic, or governance activity;
- [ ] the Beacon accepts only attributable committed contributions;
- [ ] reconnect and retry tests create no duplicate command; and
- [ ] an authored degraded mode works without model generation.

### 4.2 Content Gate

- [ ] at least seventy-two reviewed templates exist across six event families;
- [ ] each role has a first-session choice and at least eight role-relevant templates;
- [ ] at least twelve relationship episodes include consent, boundary, conflict, or repair;
- [ ] each option has an implemented consequence or valid decline state;
- [ ] no template uses emotional coercion, deceptive scarcity, or paid recovery;
- [ ] repetition, recent-actor, and unresolved-thread rules are tested;
- [ ] live operators can cancel, replace, correct, and explain a card; and
- [ ] all AI-generated language passes final factual and safety validation.

### 4.3 TOS Gate

- [ ] the exact TOS network, current protocol domain, stablecoin asset, contract identities, and resolver endpoints are recorded;
- [ ] current TOS Service readiness status is checked against its authoritative roadmap;
- [ ] no real or testnet payment screen implies production support;
- [ ] native TOS fees and stablecoin service price are separate;
- [ ] wallet or signer connection never blocks free play;
- [ ] Quote Proposal, Accepted Quote, funding, execution, Receipt, release, refund, and error states are distinct;
- [ ] ambiguous submission is resolved before retry;
- [ ] support can independently verify the complete path; and
- [ ] unavailable payment primitives remain disabled rather than simulated.

If the TOS gate does not pass, the cohort continues without a committed payment. It may test comprehension with a clearly labelled non-submittable prototype, but that prototype cannot count as willingness-to-pay or settlement evidence.

### 4.4 Safety and Governance Gate

- [ ] block, mute, report, leave, Agent suspend, moderator action, correction, and appeal work;
- [ ] incident severity, owners, response times, and stop authority are assigned;
- [ ] moderator and appeal roles are separate where practical;
- [ ] private evidence has restricted access and retention;
- [ ] campaign eligibility, office scope, conflicts, ballot, tally, challenge, and term rehearsal are tested;
- [ ] a TOS candidacy bond is disabled unless a dedicated audited testnet contract and resolver exist;
- [ ] no office has treasury, moderation, court, election-administration, or final-appeal power;
- [ ] coordinated Agents and controllers cannot multiply ballot credentials; and
- [ ] participants can distinguish community role-play from real public authority.

### 4.5 Accessibility Gate

- [ ] onboarding, Today, cards, choices, consequences, report, support, and logout pass keyboard review;
- [ ] critical states have screen-reader names, descriptions, errors, and confirmations;
- [ ] City View has a synchronized non-spatial alternative;
- [ ] reduced motion preserves all information;
- [ ] gathering video or audio has captions and a transcript;
- [ ] asynchronous participation has equal decision rights;
- [ ] text zoom and mobile layout remain usable; and
- [ ] an accessibility issue has a direct private reporting channel.

### 4.6 Internal Dry Run

Ten internal testers who did not author the relevant flow must complete a forty-eight-hour compressed season. The dry run includes onboarding, return, Circle, conflict, Beacon contribution, degraded Agent runtime, TOS resolver failure, report, moderator correction, campaign, ballot retry, and Archive close.

External launch requires a signed readiness record naming the reviewed commit, deployment, known issues, exceptions, owners, and decision time.

## 5. Staff and Responsibility

The first cohort needs named humans. Agent automation may assist but cannot replace accountable operations.

| Role | Minimum responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Cohort Director** | Final launch, pause, scope, schedule, and go/no-go decisions |
| **Game Master and Content Lead** | Event schedule, relevance, corrections, ceremonies, and content diversity |
| **Community Lead** | Welcome, Circles, gatherings, conflict facilitation, and retention interviews |
| **Safety and Moderation Lead** | Reports, protective action, evidence, privacy, and incident response |
| **Independent Appeal Reviewer** | Review consequential moderation and campaign challenges not owned by the initial decision-maker |
| **TOS Economic Lead** | Asset, signer, Quote, escrow, Receipt, resolver, support, and reconciliation |
| **Agent Operations Lead** | Runtime health, controller policy, tools, budgets, suspension, and degraded mode |
| **Research and Data Lead** | Consent, instrumentation, dashboard, interviews, analysis, and evidence labels |
| **Accessibility Lead** | Preflight, accommodations, issue triage, and parity verification |
| **Engineering On-call** | Deployment, data integrity, replay, security, and rollback or disablement |

One person may cover multiple low-conflict roles in a small team, but the initial moderator should not be the sole appeal reviewer, and a candidate should not administer the election.

### 5.1 Coverage

During announced live gatherings, provide at least:

- one Game Master;
- one safety moderator;
- one engineering or operations on-call; and
- one community facilitator.

Outside live windows, publish response expectations. Do not imply continuous human monitoring if it does not exist.

## 8. Fourteen-Day Season Schedule

### Day 0: Arrival

**Experience:** role, visual identity, sponsored AI resident, one privacy boundary, first introduction choice, Arrival Hall, and Beacon reveal.

**Human operation:** welcome windows, accessibility support, no sales, no governance lecture.

**Evidence:** onboarding completion, time to first choice, comprehension, error and support rate.

### Day 1: A Name and a Neighbor

**Experience:** the AI resident reports the result of the first introduction; one relationship episode invites a small favor or boundary.

**District development:** each role sees why another role is needed.

**Evidence:** resident-name recall, option diversity, consequence inspection.

### Day 2: First Contribution

**Experience:** a role-specific Beacon task that can be completed in five to ten minutes or delegated within scope.

**Human operation:** identify residents who have not found relevance; offer help without auto-completing the task.

**Evidence:** first earned progression, accessibility, role clarity.

### Day 3: Form a Circle

**Experience:** residents receive explained collaborator recommendations and form groups of three to six around a shared artifact or service.

**Constraint:** each Circle needs at least three contribution types.

**Evidence:** invitations, acceptance, cross-controller mix, unanswered requests.

### Day 4: A Real Tradeoff

**Experience:** Circles choose scope, deadline, privacy, attribution, or quality under limited time and commitment slots.

**Story:** an authored district constraint creates competing valid plans without a fabricated emergency.

**Evidence:** whether choices are perceived as meaningful rather than cosmetic.

### Day 5: Market Day

**Experience:** publish needs, offers, sponsorship interests, and transparent provider discovery.

**Economic rule:** browsing and proposals are free; no funds move on a Quote Proposal. An optional TOS testnet path is shown only if the readiness gate passed.

**Evidence:** offer comprehension, payment-interest intent, wallet abandonment, fraud and jargon issues.

### Day 6: Work and Culture Gathering

**Experience:** a live and asynchronous exhibition, demo, interview, or workshop combining Builder and Creator outcomes.

**Evidence:** social presence, asynchronous parity, captions, new relationships.

### Day 7: Midpoint Reflection

**Experience:** personalized week-one Archive chapter, earned changes, unresolved responsibility, and chosen week-two goal.

**Research:** short voluntary pulse and selected interviews.

**Decision:** continue, adjust content, reduce scope, or pause under the stop rules. Do not hide poor retention with extra rewards.

### Day 8: Conflict and Repair

**Experience:** a real unresolved project issue or a reviewed authored relationship episode around deadline, attribution, privacy, or responsibility.

**Required paths:** renegotiate, introduce help, reduce scope, respectfully close, or use the applicable dispute process.

**Evidence:** fairness, emotional response, moderation load, repair completion.

### Day 9: Verified Outcome

**Experience:** Circles publish a completed artifact, deliver a service, or clearly close an attempt. If enabled, one current-domain testnet TOS work lifecycle resolves through Receipt and release or refund.

**Evidence:** provenance comprehension, result usefulness, TOS state accuracy, duplicate and ambiguity handling.

### Day 10: District Direction

**Experience:** residents compare two or three next-season district proposals with scope, evidence, cost class, accessibility, and conflicts.

**Rule:** proposals may inspire the Steward campaign but do not transfer funds or grant office.

**Evidence:** civic comprehension and discussion quality.

### Day 11: Steward Candidacy

**Experience:** eligible residents publish comparable short manifestos and disclosures. A testnet TOS bond is included only if its dedicated governance gate passed.

**Human operation:** equal baseline visibility, question collection, candidate safety, conflict review.

**Evidence:** candidate diversity, bond comprehension, wealth-versus-authority confusion.

### Day 12: Debate and Ballot

**Experience:** accessible live and asynchronous questions followed by one eligible resident credential per private ballot.

**Rule:** no raw TOS balance weighting, paid ranking, candidate election administration, or Agent fleet multiplication.

**Evidence:** turnout, ballot completion, comprehension, challenge and retry handling.

### Day 13: Beacon Completion and Exhibition

**Experience:** final contributions, Circle exhibitions, transparent incomplete paths, contextual recognition, and a preview of the season Archive.

**Rule:** operators do not invent contributions to force a completed Beacon. An incomplete Beacon is valid evidence and part of the story.

**Evidence:** pride, collection value, social recognition, incomplete-goal response.

### Day 14: Closing and Steward Result

**Experience:** publish the challenged or finalized Steward result, office limits, Beacon state, season history, contributor Archive, and next-step choices.

**Research:** final survey and interview invitation. Offer no token or artificial urgency to convert.

**Evidence:** intention to return, pay, invite, create, operate an Agent, or hold responsibility—and the reason.

## 9. Daily Operations Rhythm

### Before the Resident Day

- review runtime, resolver, moderation, and accessibility health;
- inspect scheduled cards and unresolved consequences;
- check actor, template, and option repetition;
- verify every factual and economic source;
- confirm the district development and fallback;
- publish a concise status if any system is degraded; and
- assign live staff and incident owner.

### During the Day

- monitor failures, reports, inaccessible tasks, stalled Circles, and unsupported residents;
- correct false or misleading content visibly;
- avoid manually steering everyone toward the same option;
- record interventions separately from organic activity;
- keep optional gatherings within the published schedule; and
- disable a risky subsystem before trying to preserve a perfect show.

### End of Day

- reconcile choices, consequences, Focus, Beacon contributions, and TOS states;
- sample card relevance and response diversity;
- review new safety and privacy issues;
- contact residents only when support or consent permits;
- publish the next status and known issue where appropriate; and
- record one operational decision and its evidence.

## 10. Content Inventory and Fallbacks

Before launch, prepare:

- seventy-two reviewed event templates;
- five first-session choices;
- five role-specific contribution paths;
- twelve relationship episodes;
- six conflict and repair templates;
- six Circle project seeds;
- three district constraint packages;
- two gathering agendas with asynchronous versions;
- one exhibition format;
- three Steward proposal examples used only as fallback inspiration;
- onboarding, status, correction, safety, pause, and closing messages; and
- an authored no-generation briefing for every season day.

### 10.1 Fallback Hierarchy

```text
bounded generated language over validated facts
  -> authored template language over validated facts
  -> factual activity list and fixed commands
  -> read-only Archive and status
  -> safe maintenance state
```

Falling back must never invent activity, erase committed choices, or convert a proposal into a result.

## 11. TOS Economic Validation

### 11.1 Testnet-First Rule

The first cohort uses production-equivalent interface semantics but public-testnet assets unless the authoritative TOS readiness process explicitly permits a production flow. Testnet assets have no promised monetary value and must be labelled at every relevant surface.

### 11.2 One Complete Flow

If enabled, validate one narrow machine-checkable service:

```text
finalized Agent and Capability discovery
  -> Quote Proposal
  -> human review
  -> finalized Accepted Quote
  -> exact testnet stablecoin escrow funding
  -> bounded OpenFox or tos-ai execution
  -> artifact and signed Receipt
  -> finalized release or refund
  -> independently resolvable FreeCity history and visual consequence
```

Native TOS pays network fees and remains separate from the stablecoin price. Do not add tips, subscriptions, creator purchases, team splits, treasuries, or candidacy bonds to this service escrow by analogy.

### 11.3 Willingness-to-Pay Evidence

Testnet completion proves comprehension and technical usability, not willingness to spend real money. Measure willingness to pay only through one of:

- an actual lawful production purchase after readiness;
- a clearly refundable deposit under approved terms;
- a priced preorder that does not charge until stated conditions pass; or
- a product-selection experiment with a real opportunity cost approved for the cohort.

A survey answer, fake checkout, testnet token transfer, waitlist click, or speculative token interest is not paid-conversion evidence. Record intent separately.

## 12. Safety, Incident, and Stop Plan

### 12.1 Severity

| Severity | Example | Initial action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S0 Critical** | Unauthorized asset movement, exposed secret, account takeover, credible immediate harm, systemic privacy breach | Disable affected capability immediately, protect residents, preserve evidence, notify incident owners, stop cohort if scope is uncertain |
| **S1 High** | Agent exceeds scope, targeted harassment, impersonation, ballot integrity failure, sensitive-data exposure | Suspend actor or subsystem, protect target, begin review, communicate affected scope |
| **S2 Medium** | Misleading factual card, repeated unwanted contact, inaccessible critical action, unresolved project abuse | Correct or remove, provide workaround, log, review within published target |
| **S3 Low** | Irrelevant card, cosmetic defect, non-critical copy issue | Queue and monitor pattern |

### 12.2 Immediate Stop Conditions

Pause the affected subsystem or cohort when:

- an unauthorized or ambiguous asset transfer cannot be safely resolved;
- an Agent performs a high-impact action outside its authority;
- private data or memory is exposed beyond consent;
- ballot integrity, duplicate submission, or controller eligibility cannot be trusted;
- residents cannot access reporting or critical fallback;
- fabricated economic or civic activity is presented as real;
- moderation cannot protect a targeted resident;
- persistent state corruption risks history or identity; or
- staff coverage falls below the announced safety minimum during a live event.

Low retention, an incomplete Beacon, a failed project, or an unpopular event is not an emergency. Those are product evidence.

### 12.3 Communication

Incident communication states:

- what is known and unknown;
- affected function and authority class;
- immediate protective action;
- whether committed TOS or FreeCity facts remain trustworthy;
- what residents should do;
- next update time; and
- support, privacy, and appeal paths.

Do not let an Agent improvise the sole incident notice.

## 13. Research and Player Feedback

### 13.1 Behavioral Evidence

Measure:

- onboarding completion and time to first meaningful choice;
- card inspection, defer, decline, and choice diversity;
- immediate-reaction and durable-consequence viewing;
- return on distinct days;
- relationship episodes and resident-name usage;
- Circle formation and cross-controller collaboration;
- earned progression and artifact inspection;
- Beacon contribution distribution;
- payment comprehension, intent, abandonment, and valid completion;
- campaign, debate, ballot, and office-scope comprehension;
- support, accessibility, report, moderation, and appeal outcomes; and
- generation fallback and factual correction rate.

### 13.2 Short Daily Prompt

Ask at most one optional rotating question after a natural stopping point:

- Did today's choice matter to you? Why?
- What surprised you about your AI resident?
- Was any card irrelevant, repetitive, or manipulative?
- Did you understand what would happen before choosing?
- Did another resident change what you decided?
- What are you looking forward to next?

Do not interrupt every session with a full survey.

### 13.3 Midpoint Interview

Sample residents across engagement levels, roles, devices, segments, and outcomes. Include people who nearly stopped. Ask them to show, not merely describe:

- what changed while they were away;
- the most meaningful and least meaningful choice;
- who their AI resident is;
- one earned progression item;
- one relationship or Circle;
- where they would inspect payment or civic authority; and
- why they will or will not return.

### 13.4 Final Interview

Ask:

1. What would you miss if District Zero disappeared tomorrow?
2. What felt like work rather than play?
3. Which person or Agent mattered and why?
4. Which consequence felt genuinely caused by you?
5. What did you earn that you care about?
6. What would you pay for, at what cadence, and what value would that preserve?
7. What should never be sold?
8. Did TOS increase trust, create friction, or both?
9. Did the Steward campaign feel legitimate and bounded?
10. Would you invite a friend, and what would you tell them?

## 14. Metrics and Decision Gates

### 14.1 Experience Gates

| Gate | Target |
| --- | ---: |
| Accurate one-minute hook comprehension | At least 80% |
| Onboarding completion | At least 90% |
| First meaningful choice without help | At least 85% |
| Median time to first consequence | Under five minutes |
| Day 7 return among activated invited residents | At least 40% |
| Three distinct active days over two weeks | At least 55% |
| Resident named plus remembered shared episode by Day 7 | At least 60% |
| At least one earned change and next goal understood | At least 75% |
| Meaningful cross-controller social action | At least 50% |
| Completed cross-controller collaboration | At least 30% |
| Sampled cards rated irrelevant or repetitive after Day 3 | Under 10% |
| Critical task accessibility parity | 100% |

### 14.2 Trust and Economic Gates

| Gate | Target |
| --- | ---: |
| Correct fact-class comprehension in sampled tasks | At least 90% |
| Displayed committed payment with valid TOS provenance | 100% |
| Critical unauthorized payment or Agent action | Zero |
| Payment-value comprehension among exposed residents | At least 90% |
| Real or production-equivalent paid conversion after experienced value | At least 15% when a lawful ready offer exists |
| Residents who believe TOS balance directly buys Steward office | Under 5%, with immediate correction review |
| Residents who understand Steward powers and limits | At least 70% |

### 14.3 Distribution Guardrails

Review concentration, not only totals:

- contribution share by top resident, Circle, controller, and role;
- relationship and recommendation concentration;
- Agent controller concentration;
- earnings and sponsorship concentration;
- campaign attention and funding concentration;
- moderation and report concentration; and
- accessibility outcomes by device and declared need.

A completed Beacon or high transaction volume dominated by one operator is not a healthy district.

### 14.4 Outcome Decisions

#### Proceed

Proceed to a second cohort when the hook, first play, attachment, return, social, trust, safety, and accessibility gates pass, even if some secondary content or economic targets need focused iteration.

#### Iterate the Same Cohort Size

Repeat District Zero when residents understand the concept but fail on one or two core loops such as card relevance, attachment, progression, or collaboration. Change the relevant mechanic; do not add map size or governance power.

#### Narrow the Product

Narrow toward professional collaboration when useful work and payment are strong but relationship and play retention are weak. Narrow toward a social simulation when attachment and return are strong but real work causes disproportionate friction. Record the tradeoff explicitly.

#### Stop Expansion

Do not expand when safety, authority comprehension, factual integrity, or wallet boundaries fail; when residents return only for promised rewards; or when insiders are the only retained users.

## 15. Reporting

The cohort report separates:

- design targets;
- implementation readiness;
- internal dry-run evidence;
- independent versus insider cohort results;
- behavioral measurements;
- qualitative themes and contradictory evidence;
- accessibility and safety outcomes;
- TOS testnet technical evidence;
- real willingness-to-pay evidence, if any;
- operator interventions;
- failed assumptions;
- recommended product changes; and
- proceed, repeat, narrow, or stop decision.

Do not report generated messages, Agent count, wallet count, testnet volume, animated activity, or TOS price as proof of product-market fit.

## 16. After the Cohort

### First Forty-Eight Hours

- protect and reconcile durable state;
- close temporary access and excess Agent permissions;
- resolve or clearly suspend unfinished TOS flows;
- acknowledge reports and open appeals;
- publish the Archive status and data choices;
- thank residents without promising rewards; and
- avoid announcing success before analysis.

### First Week

- complete interviews;
- analyze behavioral and qualitative evidence;
- audit moderation, accessibility, Agent, generation, and TOS incidents;
- compare segments and insiders versus independent residents;
- identify the three highest-leverage changes;
- decide proceed, repeat, narrow, or stop; and
- publish an appropriate resident-facing retrospective.

### Before a Second Cohort

- update the playable specification and relevant architecture decision records;
- fix P0 failures;
- add no consequential civic power without evidence;
- add no payment primitive without its TOS contract and acceptance path;
- retire irrelevant or manipulative templates;
- test repaired flows internally; and
- explain what changed because of the first residents.

## 18. Final Go/No-Go Checklist

The Cohort Director records **GO**, **CONDITIONAL GO**, or **NO-GO** for each category:

- [ ] implemented core loop;
- [ ] internal dry run;
- [ ] content inventory;
- [ ] Agent scope and suspension;
- [ ] TOS feature accuracy;
- [ ] payment disablement or readiness;
- [ ] reporting, moderation, and appeal;
- [ ] security and incident response;
- [ ] accessibility and asynchronous parity;
- [ ] telemetry and research consent;
- [ ] staff coverage and backups;
- [ ] participant balance and informed expectations;
- [ ] degraded mode and stop controls; and
- [ ] reviewed deployment commit and configuration.

Any conditional exception names the risk, affected residents, workaround, owner, deadline, communication, and reason it does not compromise a non-negotiable invariant. There is no conditional exception for unauthorized payment risk, false economic or civic facts, missing critical reporting, or uncontrolled Agent authority.

## 19. Review Conclusion

The first cohort should not be organized as an audience for a demonstration. It should be operated as a small society whose residents can make different choices, form genuine relationships, fail, repair, create, verify, and leave.

The product team is responsible for making the first five minutes clear and the fourteen days meaningful. The cohort is responsible only for participating honestly. If residents do not return, attach, collaborate, or pay, that is evidence about the design—not a failure of the residents and not a reason to fabricate more activity.

District Zero is ready only when an implemented build passes the launch gates. When it does, this playbook gives the organizer a bounded, measurable, safe path to invite the first residents and turn the ten-out-of-ten design target into real player evidence.
